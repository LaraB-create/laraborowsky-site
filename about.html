“Who are you?” said the Caterpillar.

I follow questions further than is strictly reasonable.

Short version or the whole story. Your call.

  • Since 2012with one public-sector consulting firm, in stretches, from project-based help to marketing director.
  • 3+ yearsbuilding with AI, with a human making every final call.
  • 1 personmarketing department. Hence the systems.

Public-sector infrastructure consulting is where I’ve spent most of my career, at one small firm, working in operations, research, business development, marketing, and whatever IT problem landed on my desk that week. These days I’m a marketing and business development strategist.

I’m the whole marketing department there, which is exactly why I started building tools. When you’re one person, you either do less or you build systems that do more. I chose systems.

For the last three-plus years I’ve been building with AI: research pipelines, screening tools, content systems, a brand kit an AI can actually follow. The rule I don’t bend: the AI does the heavy lifting, and a person makes the call.

I’m openly neurodivergent, and that shows up in the work. I see systems where other people see tasks, I go deep on whatever hooks me, and I care a lot about making information easy to take in. That’s why this site lets you change the font, the spacing, and how much detail you get.

I’m still figuring a lot of this out. These are my field notes so far.

Chapter I · The path

Operations to marketing, by way of every department.

  1. 2025 to now

    Marketing Director at a public-sector consulting firm

    Brand, website, content, and the systems that keep a one-person marketing department running.

    This is where building with AI went from experiment to everyday practice, always with a person making the final call.

  2. 2023 to 2025

    Marketing Manager same firm

    Moved marketing in-house and designed just about everything with a logo on it.

    Also planned an in-person retreat for a fully remote team, travel logistics included.

  3. 2022 to 2023

    Associate Consultant same firm

    Research and pursuit analysis to support strategic planning.

    This is where the rabbit holes started: agency research, teaming partners, procurement trends.

  4. 2018 to 2019

    Business Manager same firm

    Operations, systems, and keeping the trains running.

    Learning how a firm actually runs turned out to be the best marketing training I could have had.

  5. 2012 to 2015

    Independent consultant same firm, project by project

    Built the firm’s first website, pulled together its business certification applications, and was the IT help before there was IT help.

    On and off, as projects came up. It’s how I met the firm, and how I learned that I like building the thing more than just using it.

  6. 2009 to 2011

    Client services at a call center

    One of the voices behind 50+ client contracts: pet doors, solar panels, college directory assistance, high-end butcher orders, IT help desks, restaurant delivery confirmations, and more.

    Contract lead for one of them: a wine company’s direct-to-consumer program. That meant customer service and ordering, day-to-day operations, and keeping customers, the warehouse, and the client in sync. Our team also supported the company’s first online direct-to-consumer wine sale.

  7. Since I was 15

    A little bit of everything, all of it useful

    Swim club office, lifeguard, swim teacher, camp counselor, photographer’s assistant, retail, café, and catering.

    At 15 I helped a swim club move its membership management and check-in from paper to digital. I taught swim lessons to kids, parent-and-baby classes, and a student with a disability, and lifeguarded at two pools. I assisted a school photographer, worked retail at a cooking supply store and a hat shop (where I also kept up her web store), worked the opening shift solo at a Hoboken café, making breakfast for the morning ferry commuters (lots of egg sandwiches, occasional baking included), and served weddings and events for 150+ guests at a catering hall before moving to front of house. Customer service, it turns out, is systems thinking with a smile.

Chapter III · How I work

House rules. I break them less than you’d think.

A person makes the call.

AI drafts, sorts, and screens. A human reviews and decides. Every time.

Citations or it didn’t happen.

If a tool can’t trace a claim to a source, the claim doesn’t ship.

Systems over one-offs.

If I’m doing it twice, I’m building something so I don’t do it a third time.

Accessible by default.

Built into the template, not checked at the end. If the default is accessible, nobody has to remember to be.

Fellow traveler, not guru.

I share what I’ve tried and what broke. I don’t have this all figured out, and I say so.
