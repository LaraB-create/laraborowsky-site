Chapter the First, in which a marketer follows a question somewhere it probably shouldn’t go

Six improbable things before breakfast.

Lara was beginning to get very tired of doing the same task twice, and of hunting for answers in folders with no pictures or conversations in them. So when a curious question hopped past, she followed it down the rabbit hole, and climbed back out with a tool instead of a headache.

I’m a marketing and business development strategist who builds human-in-the-loop AI tools, research systems, and the occasional wildly over-engineered spreadsheet. Curiosity, with receipts. This is where I keep them.

With apologies to Lewis Carroll.

Chapter I · Down the rabbit hole

Rabbit hole research.

It’s how I work on anything: follow the question further than is strictly reasonable, keep every receipt, then climb back out with something other people can use.

  1. 01 · 0 ft

    Fall in

    Start with the question nobody has time for, and follow it past the point where it’s comfortable.

  2. 02 · 40 ft

    Keep receipts

    Every finding gets a source. If I can’t trace it, it doesn’t go in.

  3. 03 · 80 ft

    Build the ladder

    Turn what I found into a tool someone else can use without me in the room.

  4. 04 · Surface

    Climb out

    Lead with the TL;DR. Leave the burrow open for anyone who wants the footnotes.

813.6 INDPersonal · Book events

Indies Invade Philly

Built because a signing with dozens of indie authors is a lot of books to plan for.

A preorder portal for an indie author signing event: browse every author, move books from wishlist to final picks, track preorders, and plan the big day.

How it works

The 2026 version is a single-page web app that remembers your picks in your browser. The 2027 version is in the works: a real site with logins, so friends can keep their own lists.

Margin note
